# Experience design: Lop Nur Twin

> The visitor gains an extraordinary perspective on an ordinary universe.

This is not one game with one universal fantasy. Its worlds have different claims about reality and different visitor roles. Keep those boundaries visible.

## StoryStack

- **Fantasy:** Become a careful explorer of environments, simulations, and evidence ordinarily inaccessible.
- **Action:** Explore a subject, alter a view or permitted simulation variable, inspect a consequence, and investigate its basis.
- **Economy:** Time, information, provenance, confidence, and uncertainty. Evidence is earned by records, not points or spectacle.
- **World:** The evidence-marked Lop Nur reconstruction, Bethesda, R.A.I.N., Noeterra, Blacksite, Eva, and ECHO.
- **Story:** A visit becomes a question, observation, comparison, and often a better question.

## Distinct experiential contracts

- **Lop Nur evidence twin:** fantasy of a field investigator. Select a structure; compare evidence statuses or timeline dates; inspect sources. A display setting does not change the historical evidence.
- **Bethesda:** fantasy of an urban observer. Navigate a simulated city, discover changes in its systems, and distinguish model rules from observations about the real city.
- **Eva and ECHO:** fantasy of witnessing independent digital organisms. Their continuity and saved histories matter more than eliciting a response to a visitor. Never reset identity or imply biological sentience.
- **Noeterra:** fantasy of a ruler acting inside a rule-based counterfactual history. Decisions and their consequences drive the experience; labels must separate invented politics from historical facts.
- **Blacksite:** fantasy of an agent navigating a fictional situation. Preserve the explicit fiction label and avoid presenting gameplay as site evidence.
- **R.A.I.N. Lab:** fantasy of observing scientific inquiry. Let visitors trace hypotheses, experiment conditions, results, failures, and revisions instead of presenting AI output as settled discovery.

## First-visit loop

1. **Explore:** show a real interactive object or record immediately, with accessible non-WebGL routes for the twin.
2. **Influence:** let the visitor choose a view, timeline, controlled experimental condition, or game decision. Never imply agency over a persistent organism when the visitor merely observes it.
3. **Discover:** expose the visible or measured consequence, supporting record, limitations, and a natural next question.
4. **Explore again:** preserve state when appropriate and make the next comparison reachable without repeating the opening.

## Review criteria

A newcomer should be able to name the chosen world's purpose, perform its central action on desktop or mobile, tell simulation from evidence, and reach a checkable record. No fake discoveries, fabricated measurements, invented provenance, or cosmetic interactivity. Respect determinism, model history, accessibility, and the existing route contracts.
