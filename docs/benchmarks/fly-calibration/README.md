# Digital fly calibration

Generated by `node scripts/run-ts.mjs scripts/fly-calibration.ts` from aa2c65e (digital-fly/8); do not edit. It sets the digital fly beside published recordings of real _Drosophila_: every observation in `src/bethesda/fly/calibration/observations.ts` and the 2026-11 held-out set in `holdout.ts`, each paradigm rebuilt by `paradigms.ts` or `holdoutParadigms.ts` and run by `report.ts` on three controllers: the reduced-order one (Reduced-order behavioural controller), the circuit-informed one (Circuit-informed controller) and its successor (Circuit-informed controller v2), which adds five behavioural mechanisms, each also switched off in turn (ablations). It describes a simulator, never flies: each comparison inherits its paradigm's approximations, listed below.

How to read a row. _Role_, per controller (`roles.ts`): **held-out** (nothing in that controller was set from it; for the 2026-11 set, it was registered and sealed before circuit-informed/v2 was committed or run on its paradigms, and v2 was never run on it before this report), **design** (a parameter came from it, so agreement is by construction), **fit** (a fitted value of that controller was set to match it) or **development** (circuit-informed/v2 only: seen while v2 was built, so agreement is not evidence for v2). _Verdict_: **inside** the 95% interval of the published central value, **within spread** of the published flies (± 1 SD, the IQR or the range), **outside** both, **no interval** when the paper gives no spread to judge by (compare the numbers), **agrees**/**disagrees** for a reported direction, **not produced** when the simulated fly does not show the behaviour at all. Simulated intervals are 95% intervals of the estimate (normal for means, Wilson for proportions).

## The 2026-11 held-out set

Registered from papers the calibration had not used, and sealed (`holdout-2026-11.json` beside this file, with its disclosure of the order of work) before circuit-informed/v2 was committed or run on any of their paradigms. Nothing in any controller was set from these, and v2 was run on their paradigms for the first time for this report. They are the test of whether v2's mechanisms generalize beyond the observations they were developed on. circuit-informed/v2's sources are byte for byte (line endings aside) those the held-out set was sealed against.

The metrics and acceptance rules declared in the seal: the primary count is the observations with a published interval on which the estimate is inside it or within the flies' spread; the secondary metric is the median absolute relative error over observations without one (an observation not produced counting as 1).

## Paradigms

### Looming disk over a standing fly (`vonReyn2014`, von Reyn et al. 2014, Nat Neurosci 17:962)

Experiment: Single unrestrained control flies on a platform; a dark disk expanding from 15° to 90° at constant r/v (8, 14, 40, 70 or 140 ms), at 45° elevation with a frontward azimuth bias; one stimulus per fly; filmed at 6000 frames/s.

Rebuilt: A fly standing at rest in a lit, otherwise empty 4 × 2.5 × 4 m room, spontaneous takeoff, walking and grooming off, one looming stimulus aimed at it after 1 s: 15° → 90° at the published r/v, 45° elevation, azimuth drawn uniformly within ±60° of straight ahead, then held at 90° for 1 s. A trial ends when the hold does.

Approximations:

- The paper reports the azimuth as '±60° for over 90% of flies'; here it is uniform within ±60°.
- The hold at 90° is not stated for the freely behaving flies; the paper's tethered experiments held 90° for 1 s, which is used here.
- The disk is shown as a sphere moved so that it subtends the disk's angle at every moment (`stimuli.ts`): the angular size and expansion are the published ones; the luminance change is not modelled.
- The platform and dome are not modelled; the fly stands on an open floor.

### Looming square beside a standing fly (`fotowat2009`, Fotowat et al. 2009, J Neurophysiol 102:875)

Experiment: Wild-type flies at the tip of a funnel, a dark square approaching on a collision course on a monitor beside them (monocular in 65% of trials), l/|v| from 5 to 80 ms; filmed at 400 frames/s.

Rebuilt: As the looming-disk paradigm, with the stimulus at 90° azimuth and 0° elevation, growing from 5° to 150° at l/v 10 or 40 ms and held for 0.2 s.

Approximations:

- The square on a flat monitor is shown as a disk-equivalent sphere; the published square grew until the projected collision, here it stops at 150°.
- The funnel is not modelled.

### Looming threat from the front, side or back (`card2008`, Card & Dickinson 2008, Curr Biol 18:1300)

Experiment: Females on a 5 mm² platform; a real black disk of 70 mm radius falling toward them along a 50° path from the front, side or back; filmed at 5400 frames/s.

Rebuilt: As the looming-disk paradigm at r/v 40 ms, 10° → 90°, 45° elevation, from straight ahead, the side (90°) or straight behind (180°).

Approximations:

- A constant-r/v loom stands for the falling disk, whose approach was not at constant speed.

### Repeated looms over a walking fly (`zacarias2018`, Zacarias et al. 2018, Nat Commun 9:3697)

Experiment: Single mated females in a covered arena 30 mm across and 4 mm high, 5 min to explore, then 20 looms over 5 min on a monitor angled above the arena: a disk of 1 cm at 25 cm/s (l/v 40 ms) expanding over 450 ms to 78°, held 50 ms.

Rebuilt: The fly in a clear round arena 30 mm across and 4 mm high with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off and its other behaviour its own, 5 min to explore, then 20 looms 15 s apart, each aimed at where it is from a direction drawn within ±90° of its heading, 45° up: 9.2° → 78° over 450 ms (l/v 40 ms), held 50 ms. A jump is a takeoff within 1 s of a loom's onset.

Approximations:

- The schedule within the 5 min is not stated here; the looms are spaced evenly.
- The monitor angled above the arena is not modelled: the fly's eyes (FlyBody's two 150° cones) do not cover the zenith, and a fixed direction would often be hidden by the 4 mm wall from a fly walking along it, so each loom comes from the fly's frontal half, 45° up, through clear walls and lid.
- The 9.2° start is the size 450 ms before 78° at l/v 40 ms.
- The lid keeps the fly in the arena: it lands again after a jump.

### Dusted and undusted flies alone in a chamber (`zhang2020`, Zhang, Guo & Simpson 2020, Curr Biol 30:988)

Experiment: Canton S males, 4–7 days old, shaken 10 times in a dusting chamber with Reactive Yellow 86 dust or, as the undusted control, in chambers without dust, then filmed alone at 30 Hz for 50 000 frames (27.78 min) at 21–23 °C; frames labelled by the Automatic Behavior Recognition System classifier (Zhang, Guo & Simpson 2020, STAR Methods).

Rebuilt: The fly alone in a closed square chamber 15.6 mm across and 4 mm high, dusted all over (the grooming circuit's dust as the model's authors set it) or not, spontaneous takeoff off; its behaviour labelled at 30 Hz for 50 000 frames (1–5 grooming routines, 6 walking, 7 standing), as many flies as the data have, and the labels reduced by the same code as the recorded ethograms (`ethogram.ts`).

Approximations:

- The chamber width is Seeds et al. 2014's (the same laboratory's 15.6 mm chambers); its height is a model choice.
- Real dust is not modelled: a dusted fly carries the grooming circuit's dust from the start, and the world adds none.
- The simulated label is the fly's own mode, not a classifier's reading of video; standing includes resting.
- The fly's notum cleaning, which the data have no label for, counts as back-leg rubbing.

### Free flight in a 1 m-radius arena (`censi2013`, Censi et al. 2013, PLoS Comput Biol 9:e1002891)

Experiment: 88 flies tracked at 60 frames/s in a circular arena of 1 m radius with a retro-illuminated checkerboard wall; only samples with flight speed above 5 cm/s kept; saccades found by the authors' geometric detector.

Rebuilt: The fly released in cruise at the centre of a round arena of 1 m radius and 1 m height, 0.5 m up, heading drawn at random, and followed until it lands or for 60 s; many releases. Positions at 60 Hz; the heading of travel from successive positions; airborne samples faster than 5 cm/s kept; a saccade is a maximal run of samples turning one way faster than 300°/s.

Approximations:

- The arena's height is not stated here; 1 m is a model choice.
- The authors' detectors (their Text S1) are not reproduced; 300°/s is Tammero & Dickinson's and van Breugel & Dickinson's threshold.
- The checkerboard wall is a plain lit wall: the fly's senses have no texture.
- The fly's preferred cruise height, a model value sized for the city (0.3–2.5 m), is set to 0.1–0.6 m for the arena, and its extent's ceiling sits 0.3 m above the lid, where the controller's rule of keeping 0.3 m below it holds the fly under the lid.

### Free flight in a 1 m cylinder (`tammero2002a`, Tammero & Dickinson 2002, J Exp Biol 205:327)

Experiment: Flies tracked at 30 frames/s in a translucent cylinder 1 m across and 0.6 m high lined with a random checkerboard.

Rebuilt: As the 1 m-radius arena, in a round arena 1 m across and 0.6 m high, the fly released 0.3 m up; positions at 30 Hz and every airborne sample kept.

Approximations:

- The checkerboard lining is a plain lit wall: the fly's senses have no texture.
- The fly's preferred cruise height is set to 0.1–0.4 m for the arena, its extent's ceiling 0.3 m above the lid.

### Landing on, or flying past, a post (`vanBreugel2012`, van Breugel & Dickinson 2012, J Exp Biol 215:1783)

Experiment: Females in the still-air section (1.5 × 0.3 × 0.3 m) of a wind tunnel with a checkerboard floor and walls and a post 15 cm tall and 1.9 cm across in the centre; 3D tracking at 100 frames/s; landings and fly-bys among the trajectories coming within 3 cm of the post.

Rebuilt: The fly released in cruise at a random point of a closed 1.5 × 0.3 × 0.3 m tunnel (5–25 cm up, at least 10 cm from the post), heading drawn at random, and followed until it lands or for 10 s, positions at 100 Hz; a post 15 cm tall and 1.9 cm across stands in the centre. A trajectory counts when it comes within 3 cm of the post's surface; it is a landing if the fly touches down on the post. Speeds are the body's at the last crossing of 10 cm from the post before touching down (landings) or before the closest approach (fly-bys).

Approximations:

- The simulated fly lands only on tops, so it lands on the post's 1.9 cm top; the real flies landed on its side as well. Its leg extension is set beside the retinal size of that top.
- The fly is released inside the section; the real flies entered it on their own.
- The checkerboard is a plain lit surface: the fly's senses have no texture.
- The fly's preferred cruise height is set to 0.05–0.25 m for the tunnel, its extent's ceiling 0.3 m above the lid.

### Open field 9.1 cm across (`liu2007`, Liu, Davis & Roman 2007, Genetics 175:1197)

Experiment: Canton-S males alone in a clear circular arena 9.1 cm across and 0.7 cm high, positions every 100 ms for 10 min.

Rebuilt: The fly placed at rest at a random point of a round arena 9.1 cm across and 0.7 cm high with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off, positions every 100 ms for 3 min. Moving is the walking mode; the outermost third is the outer third of the area (r ≥ 0.8165 R).

Approximations:

- The tracker's threshold for moving is not stated; the fly's walking mode stands for it.
- Whether the outermost third is by area is inferred from the paper's 'A fly moving about at random may be expected to spend ∼33% of the time within this outer region'.

### Circular arena of 4.2 cm radius (`soibam2012`, Soibam et al. 2012, PLoS ONE 7:e46570)

Experiment: Canton-S flies in a circular arena of 4.2 cm radius and 0.7 cm height, tracked at 30 frames/s; trajectories sampled every 1/6 s; turn angles from the central zone, more than 6 mm from the wall.

Rebuilt: The fly placed at rest at a random point of a round arena of 4.2 cm radius and 0.7 cm height with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off, positions every 1/6 s for 10 min; a turn angle is the change of direction between successive displacements that both lie in the central zone, a displacement under 0.1 mm counting as none.

Approximations:

- The sampling interval and the three angles are equation images in the paper's text; they are read from those images.
- The minimum displacement that has a direction is not stated; 0.1 mm is a model choice.

### Leg coordination against walking speed (`mendes2013`, Mendes et al. 2013, eLife 2:e00231)

Experiment: Oregon R flies walking straight in a 7 × 40 mm glass tunnel, footprints imaged at 250 frames/s; frames classed by which legs are in stance.

Rebuilt: The body's gait (`gait.ts`) evaluated at 200 evenly spaced phases of its cycle at every 0.1 mm/s of each speed class within the body's walking range (7.2–40 mm/s): the share of instants in tripod coordination. The gait is the body's, so both controllers share it.

Approximations:

- The real flies' speeds within each class are not stated; the speeds are spread evenly.
- Tripod is three legs in stance in the alternating triplets; the paper's classification of frames is not reproduced.

### Stance duration against walking speed (`deAngelis2019`, DeAngelis, Zavatone-Veth & Clark 2019, eLife 8:e46409)

Experiment: Groups of wild-type females walking spontaneously in a 5 cm arena 2.5 mm high at 34 °C, filmed at 150 frames/s; stance durations fitted against forward speed by a power law.

Rebuilt: The body's stance duration (duty factor × step period, `gait.ts`) at 10, 20 and 30 mm/s set beside the paper's fitted law at those speeds.

Approximations:

- The comparison is with the paper's fit (R² = 0.59), not with its data.

### Looms over a fly alone in a slanted arena (`ferreira2020`, Ferreira & Moita 2020, Nat Commun 11)

Experiment: Mated Canton-S females, 4–6 days old, alone in an arena 68 mm across (flat centre 32 mm, 11° slanted wall) and 5 mm thick, filmed from above at 60 Hz: a 5 min baseline, then twenty 500 ms looms every 10–20 s from a 144 Hz monitor tilted 45° over the stage (a black circle, l/v 40 ms). Freezing is a 500 ms period with a median pixel change below 30 pixels in the fly's region.

Rebuilt: The fly at rest at a random point of a clear round arena 68 mm across and 5 mm high with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off and its other behaviour its own, 5 min of baseline, then 20 looms, each aimed at the fly from a direction drawn within ±90° of its heading, 45° up, growing from 9.2° to 78° over 450 ms (l/v 40 ms) and held 50 ms, as in the rebuilt Zacarias et al. paradigm, each followed by a gap drawn uniformly from 10–20 s; the stimulation period is the 20 looms and their gaps. A 500 ms bin is freezing when the fly is at rest (neither walking, grooming nor in the air) at every 60 Hz sample in it; the paper's pixel-change classifier counts any such stillness, so the reduced-order and v1 controllers' rest counts too. Per fly: the share of the period's bins freezing, and, of the looms after which the fly is freezing in the first bin after the loom ends, the share after which it is not freezing in some later bin before the next loom (an exit). Medians across flies.

Approximations:

- The slanted wall is not modelled: the floor is flat to the 68 mm wall.
- The monitor tilted over the stage is not modelled; each loom comes from the fly's frontal half, 45° up, through a clear wall and lid, for the reasons given for Zacarias et al.'s paradigm.
- The number of single flies is not in the text; 60 are run, as for Zacarias et al.
- The paper reports the values with interquartile ranges but does not say they are medians; they are compared as medians across flies.
- An exit is read from the paper's 'the probability of stopping freezing, i.e., resuming movement, before the following stimulus presentation'; flies never freezing after a loom have no exit probability and are left out of that median.

### Sixty looms over a fly in a small arena (`barrios2021`, Barrios, Farias & Moita 2021, Curr Biol 31)

Experiment: Mated Canton-S males, 10–11 days old, alone in arenas 16 mm across with 4 mm to move above 1% agar; 60 looms over 15 min from a 240 Hz monitor tilted 45° over the stage, each 500 ms: expanding for 450 ms at l/v 40 ms to 78°, held 50 ms.

Rebuilt: The fly at rest at a random point of a clear round arena 16 mm across and 4 mm high with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off, 5 min to explore, then 60 looms 15 s apart, each aimed at the fly from a direction drawn within ±90° of its heading, 45° up, growing from 9.2° to 78° over 450 ms (l/v 40 ms) and held 50 ms, as in the rebuilt Zacarias et al. paradigm; the stimulation period is the 15 min from the first loom's onset. A 500 ms bin is freezing when the fly is at rest (neither walking, grooming nor in the air) at every 60 Hz sample in it; the paper's pixel-change classifier counts any such stillness, so the reduced-order and v1 controllers' rest counts too. Per fly the share of the period's bins freezing; the mean across flies.

Approximations:

- Whether the flies had time to explore before the looms is not in the quoted text; 5 min is used, as in Zacarias et al. and Ferreira & Moita from the same laboratory.
- The spacing of the 60 looms within the 15 min is not stated; they are spaced evenly.
- The monitor tilted over the stage is not modelled; each loom comes from the fly's frontal half, 45° up, through a clear wall and lid, for the reasons given for Zacarias et al.'s paradigm.
- The freezing classifier is not quoted for this paper; Ferreira & Moita's 500 ms bins (same laboratory) are used.
- The number of flies behind the value is not stated next to it; 60 are run.

### Runs and stops of a starved fly (`jung2015`, Jung, Hueston & Bhandawat 2015, eLife 4)

Experiment: Single starved (18–24 h) females in a ring 6.4 cm across and 3 mm high over a mesh floor with radial airflow (1.2 L/min in, 6 L/min out), 30 min to acclimatize, then 3 min without odour (the 'before' period), filmed at 30 frames/s. Below 0.5 mm/s a fly is at a stop, above 1 mm/s on a run, and in between it continues the previous one.

Rebuilt: The fly at rest at a random point of a round arena 6.4 cm across and 3 mm high with a lid, placed with a tenth of a full reserve (hungry), spontaneous takeoff off; 30 min unrecorded, then 3 min at 30 frames/s. Speed is the frame-to-frame displacement of the body's centre; runs and stops by the paper's thresholds and rule. Medians over all flies' stops and runs, and over runs of each run's mean speed.

Approximations:

- The airflow and mesh floor are not modelled.
- Starvation: 18–24 h at the model's resting rate would empty the reserve (the model stores no fat), and an empty reserve stops voluntary movement, so the fly is placed with a tenth of a full reserve instead.
- Stops and runs cut by the start or end of the 3 min are kept as the period gives them; the paper does not say whether it dropped them.
- That the run speed is a median over runs of each run's speed is read from 'speed during runs (Figure 2F, median speed ...)'.
- 60 flies are run, as in the paper.

### Walking in an arena with heated walls (`cruz2021`, Cruz, Pérez & Chiappe 2021, Curr Biol 31)

Experiment: Wing-clipped Top Banana males, 2–4 days old, alone in a circular arena 90 mm across under a ceiling, with walls heated to 42 °C so that they do not follow the perimeter, tracked at 60 Hz. Activity is translational speed above 0.5 mm/s or angular speed above 20°/s for at least 166 ms; walking bouts are activity lasting at least 333 ms.

Rebuilt: The fly at rest at a random point of a round arena 90 mm across and 3 mm high with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off (clipped wings), 9.7 min at 60 Hz. Translational speed from frame-to-frame displacement, angular speed from the body's yaw; activity and walking bouts by the paper's thresholds. Per fly the share of time in walking bouts; the mean across flies.

Approximations:

- The heated walls are not modelled, so the fly can follow the wall, which the heat prevents in the experiment.
- The ceiling's height is not in the quoted text; 3 mm is used.
- The session length is not stated per fly; 9.7 min is the quoted 441 min of walking over 54% of the time and 84 flies.
- Whether light and dark trials are pooled is not stated; the arena is lit.
- 84 flies are run, as in the paper.

### Fifteen minutes in a small dish (`iyengar2012`, Iyengar et al. 2012, J Neurogenet 26)

Experiment: Four Canton-S males, 3–6 days old, together in a 35 mm petri dish over food with about 2 mm of headspace, filmed at 30 frames/s for 15 min starting within 10 s of introduction. A fly is moving when its speed exceeds 3 mm/s.

Rebuilt: One fly at rest at a random point of a round arena 35 mm across and 2 mm high with a lid, spontaneous takeoff off, recorded at 30 frames/s for 15 min from placement. Speed from frame-to-frame displacement; the share of frames above 3 mm/s, the median speed over moving frames pooled across flies, and the summed displacement, each averaged (the median pooled) across flies.

Approximations:

- Each fly is alone; the paper's four males share a dish and may interact.
- The food under the flies is not modelled.
- That the median speed is over moving frames only is inferred from the figure legend quoted with it.
- 40 flies are run, as in the paper.

### Free flight in a still-air wind tunnel (`stupski2024`, Stupski & van Breugel 2024, Curr Biol 34)

Experiment: Starved (6–8 h) Heisenberg-Canton-S females, 2–5 days old, in groups of 15, flying freely in a wind tunnel with a 1.0 × 0.5 × 0.5 m working section in still air, tracked at 100 Hz; a trajectory entering a trigger zone gets a stimulus or, in sham trials, none, and saccades from 0.9–3 s after the trigger are counted by a geometric detector, as a frequency per trajectory.

Rebuilt: One fly released in cruise at the centre of a closed box 1.0 × 0.5 × 0.5 m, with a reserve 7 h at the model's resting rate leaves (0.55 of full), recorded at 100 Hz; the sham trigger is the release. Saccades are counted from 0.9–3 s after it by the calibration's detector (runs of samples whose heading of travel turns faster than 300°/s, flight faster than 5 cm/s), in flights that stay airborne through the window; per flight the count over 2.1 s. Mean and SD across flights.

Approximations:

- The trigger zone is not modelled; the release stands for the sham trigger.
- The paper's geometric saccade detector is replaced by the calibration's angular-velocity detector.
- Each fly flies alone; the paper's flies are in groups of 15.
- Starvation is the reserve the model's resting rate leaves after 7 h.
- The number of trajectories is not stated (648 saccades); 150 flights are run.

### Free flight in a long arena (`straw2011`, Straw et al. 2011, J R Soc Interface 8)

Experiment: Twenty females released together into a 0.3 × 0.3 × 1.5 m arena whose long walls and floor carry a 5 cm checkerboard, tracked at 100 frames/s for 12 h; horizontal speed per frame, pooled over the frames of flight 5 cm or more from every surface, at high contrast.

Rebuilt: One fly released in cruise at the centre of a closed box 0.3 × 0.3 × 1.5 m (long axis horizontal), recorded at 100 Hz for one flight (to its first landing, at most 60 s). Horizontal speed from frame-to-frame displacement on frames 5 cm or more from every surface; the mean over frames pooled across flights.

Approximations:

- The checkerboard is not modelled; the fly's speed does not depend on the contrast of what it sees.
- Each fly flies alone, and each flight is a fresh fly; the paper pools twenty flies over 12 h without identities.
- The SD is over frames, which are not independent, so no interval of the mean is formed from it; the comparison is against the spread.
- 150 flights are run.

### Passes through a chamber's centre (`maya2026`, Maya et al. 2026, J Exp Biol 229)

Experiment: 10–30 Canton-S females, 3–7 days old, free to fly in a chamber with food; four cameras at 16,000 frames/s film a cube 3.5 cm on a side at the chamber's centre, 10 cm from its floor and side walls; a flight event is a pass through that volume (typically 180–250 ms) and its speed the event's mean.

Rebuilt: One fly released in cruise at a random point of a closed box 23.5 cm on each side, recorded at 1000 Hz for one flight (to its first landing, at most 60 s). Each pass through the central 3.5 cm cube is an event; its speed the mean over its frames. Mean and SD across events.

Approximations:

- The chamber's size is inferred from the cube's place, 10 cm from the floor and side walls: 23.5 cm on each side.
- The food is not modelled, and each fly flies alone.
- Frames are taken at 1000 Hz rather than 16,000 Hz; the event's mean speed is the same to well within the spread.
- Events from one flight are pooled as the paper pools events of unidentified flies; 3000 flights are run, since few flights cross the central cube.

### Spontaneous saccades on a magnetic tether (`mongeau2017`, Mongeau & Frye 2017, Curr Biol 27)

Experiment: Females 3–5 days old flying on a pin in a magnetic field, free to turn in yaw only, in a stationary random-patterned surround; yaw filmed at 160 frames/s. A saccade is detected where angular velocity exceeds four times the noise floor; its period is where angular velocity exceeds a quarter of its peak; saccades below 10° or above 180° are rejected.

Rebuilt: Free flight in the rebuilt Censi et al. arena, recorded at 160 Hz; the body's yaw from each frame. A saccade is a run of samples whose yaw rate exceeds 300°/s in one direction; its period the samples around its peak where the yaw rate exceeds a quarter of the peak; its amplitude the yaw change over the period. Saccades below 10° or above 180° are rejected. Medians over all saccades.

Approximations:

- The fly flies freely, translating as well as turning; the paper's fly can only turn. Only yaw is compared.
- The simulated yaw has no measurement noise, and the paper does not give its noise floor's value, so detection uses Tammero & Dickinson's 300°/s.
- 150 flights are run.
